# Project brief: research guidance alpha

The full brief as supplied on 7 October 2026. A condensed version is in `CLAUDE.md`.

## Background and goals

The National Archives has been supporting people to access our collections online for decades. We have a world leading Collections Expertise and Engagement (CEE) department, who support users every day to find and understand the records we hold. Over this time there have been several approaches to research guidance and now we have nearly 400 research guides with various types of content and styles.

Over the years we have also developed a successful principle: staff and users alike have access to the same information about the collection - a shared view of shared content. This supports staff to show people how to teach themselves more about our collection. It means that research guides play a central role in how staff familiarise themselves with new to them parts of the collection as well as users.

In recent years we've seen an increase in public enquiries that have been mis-led by AI summaries which distort the ability for staff and users to have a shared view, and land users deeper into the research journey than google search.

We ran a discovery phase in March 26. It confirmed several challenges with the existing approach that 1. research guidance was distant (and hard to find) from point of need, 2. that the content was difficult to parse 3. content was inconsistently written, marked up and poorly maintained (contributing to poor AI summaries).

In alpha we need to learn what the right thing to make is for two broad patterns 1. In context support and signposting, 2. long form progressively detailed support for users and machines alike.

### Goal

Support users to teach themselves how to find and understand records, step by step, from wherever they start.

### Hypothesis

1. If we provide the most important support content at point of need then users will be able to adapt and refine better research tactics
2. If we create a consistent predictable template that supports users and agents to progressively get into the detail of a topic then users and agents will be able to find the level of detail they need to take the next step
3. If we have reusable content support components then it will be easier to maintain as the collection, technology and user needs change

### Challenges

1. AI summaries and quick answers can mislead users and are changing user behaviour at a fast pace
2. Research guidance and support is hard for users to find
3. Large and varied amount of support content. There are many different types and approaches within the existing research guidance
4. TNA Search mechanics are constantly evolving with a new search planned to launch in October
5. TNA collection is constantly growing (rapid increase in born digital records)
6. High expectations that our records are digitised and that you can search the text of the records (or that our descriptions are consistent).
7. Low awareness of the nature of archives and archival research
8. Working with collection experts who are new to agile and digital ways of working
9. The biggest risk to this phase is scope creep so we will need to be focused on the outcomes for each phase
   1. Catalogue, website homepage, Explore the Collection and guided search are out of scope for this phase

## About the work

We are looking for a content strategist to join the dots. They will work across support in context and longer form support to make sure content is progressive and not repetitive or laborious. They will work with 2 content designers and collection experts to mark up the types of support content and develop a content model made up of reusable content components. They will help establish an effective evaluation criteria for support content. This approach will need to scale to support auditing and migration of all research guidance in Beta phase into the new designs. It will also be the building blocks for a maintenance strategy to keep content up to date and robust over changing contexts.

The senior interaction designer will focus on designing a pattern for long term support content. This will need to keep to the principle of shared content while meeting the needs of users looking to learn vs troubleshoot their research. They will need to work closely with user researchers to map how they will balance these competing needs as users navigate and orientate through a large volume of support content. They will also need to work closely with the lead interaction designer on support in context to support users to move freely between support and functionality or services.

### Phase 1: Ice sculpting (throw away prototypes) - 5 weeks

Outcome: Understanding the most effective approaches for users

For the first five weeks we will pick 5 areas to focus on, one for each week e.g. early modern period. We will design the best in context and long form prototypes for that area. Our ideas will need to be grounded in the possible but not constrained by existing solutions. The purpose of these prototypes is to help us learn more about the problem and experiment with new ideas about what could help and have impact on users. We can experiment with different content styles as well as interactive designs to learn more about what is effective for a range of users.

Example "Ice Sculpting" week schedule (TBC)

- Day 1: Review prioritised research guides for that domain and markup paragraphs (content and CEE)
- Day 2: Define problem and ideate solutions through co-design (all team incl CEE)
- Day 3: Design and build prototype (designers) - CEE colleague continue with reviewing and marking up research guides
- Day 4: Test (user research and all team)
- Day 5: Evaluate, retro and document (all team)

### Phase 2: Prioritisation and refinement - 5 weeks

Outcome: Effective and coherent set of refined design patterns and content guidance

This phase will start with a week reviewing and reflecting on what we have learned in the Ice sculpting phase. By the end of week 6 we will need to prioritise a few ideas that could work across different areas and needs to take forward and refine over 2 sprints.

## Outcomes and deliverables

### Content strategist

Strategic focus across in context support and long form support

1. Continue to mark up existing types of content in long form support (research guides)
2. Review information architecture
3. Content model with reusable content components (that progressively discloses detail)
   1. Template for longer form support content
   2. Labelling for signposting and in context support text
4. Evaluation criteria or standard for research guides
   1. Named owners for different types of content
   2. Must reflect how content may be used by agents as well as users
5. Style guide to take forward into beta
6. Half day of introductory training for collection experts
7. Short summary report and presentation at the end of each phase of work done

### Senior interaction designer

Delivery focus on long form support

8. Set of rapid interactive low-fi prototypes that represent different concepts
   1. 5 different areas
   2. 3 different modes of use (e.g. trouble shooting vs first time introduction)
9. Refined prototypes using component library
   1. New design components as needed with rules for their use
10. User journeys (working with user researchers) for "quick answer" vs "investigative" user groups
    1. Map entry points
    2. Navigation and orientation through progressive support content that works for both user groups
11. Short summary report and presentation at the end of each phase (ice sculpting and prioritisation).

## Ways of working

- Regular updates to the CEE and Digital Services department
- Collaborating with a multidisciplinary agile team with collection expert embedded in the team during ice sculpting weeks
  - In particular user research, content design and interaction design will need to work closely to balance progressive approach with speed of finding the necessary detail
- GDPR compliance and secure data handling
- Conform to the civil service code
